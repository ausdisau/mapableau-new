# Australian local-government data atlas references

**Review date:** 28 November 2026, Australia/Sydney.

**Status:** Verified source documentation; proposed context integration. No additional dataset, demographic join or live atlas connection was implemented.

## Interpretation and current state

The phrase “Local Government Australia Data Atlas” does not identify a unique product in the official sources checked. It may refer to a national atlas, ABS regional statistics or local-government boundary information. The candidates below are verified services; none is presented as a confirmed match for that exact title.

MapAble already has source-reported facility points, a reviewed NSW council-boundary snapshot and official council references. An atlas can help participants and council planners investigate area context, compare information gaps and locate additional authoritative sources. It cannot establish whether a particular participant can use a facility or whether a council owns it. The existing [council linkage](community-infrastructure-councils.md) remains the implementation baseline.

## Publication date is not geography vintage

ABS Data by region retains the **2011–25** reference-period title and an initial 11 November 2025 date, but its inspected page records an update on **10 November 2026**. Its LGA data still uses **LGA 2021 boundaries, ASGS Edition 3**; later council-boundary changes are not represented. Its geospatial services provide the latest data year from the annual November release, while tables contain series with differing periods and definitions. Check the selected item rather than treating the page update as every observation's date. [ABS methodology](https://www.abs.gov.au/methodologies/data-region-methodology/2011-25)

The separate [ASGS Edition 4 LGA page](https://www.abs.gov.au/statistics/standards/australian-statistical-geography-standard-asgs/edition-4-july-2026-june-2031/non-abs-structures/local-government-areas), released **14 October 2026**, describes annually updated Mesh Block approximations for statistical use. ABS explicitly distinguishes these from official local-government boundaries. Publishing Edition 4 does not change the geography underpinning the inspected Data by region series.

## Proposed architecture and acceptance gates

Keep statistical context separate from the GAIS facility evidence contract. A future context record should retain publisher, dataset/layer ID, version, exact query, geography type/code/edition, observation period, measure/unit, denominator, flags, source retrieval time and licence. If Digital Atlas exposes an ABS series already represented through the ABS Data API, reconcile it to that source identity rather than treating the two portals as independent corroboration.

Before adding a context view, the analyst and geospatial maintainer should:

1. Choose the specific planning question and measure, then inspect its metadata, codes and bounded response. Preserve missing, suppressed and not-stated values.
2. Verify matching geography and infrastructure coverage. Where a correspondence is necessary, have its assumptions and effect on the result reviewed.
3. Archive release-specific licence and attribution evidence. [ABS copyright guidance](https://www.abs.gov.au/website-privacy-copyright-and-disclaimer) provides CC BY 4.0 with exceptions; atlas discovery does not remove third-party or product-specific terms. Keep the existing OSM/NSW boundary licences distinct.
4. Require human review before describing an apparent gap as a provision problem. Missing mapping, different dates, cross-boundary travel and incomplete facility attributes can explain a pattern.

The [ABS context brief](community-infrastructure-abs-context.md) supplies the existing candidate and retrieval limitations. Census core activity need for assistance must not be relabelled as all disability prevalence, wheelchair demand or an individual's access requirements. Likewise, the Digital Atlas description's Service Accessibility Explorer concerns road distance/driving time to services; it does not establish a step-free route or building accessibility. ## Privacy, accessibility, tests and recovery

Use public aggregate context. No personal health record, participant address or travel history is needed; participant research retains its own consent requirements. Do not infer personal need from the area where someone lives. Offer an equivalent keyboard/screen-reader table with clear periods, units and unknown values alongside any map; do not communicate uncertainty only through colour.

Future deterministic tests should reject mismatched geography editions, duplicate identities, unsupported units and absent denominators; preserve source flags; and distinguish a failed response from zero. Security controls should bound queries and validate responses before publication. Keep a source/query audit trail and make any modelled allocation explicit.

A new atlas adapter must fail independently of facility discovery. On failure, retain the last reviewed, dated context with a stale label or show context unavailable. Rollback removes the derived context without modifying OSM evidence or council associations. The present change adds only documentation and a related-doc link; strict technical validation checks its structure, not a live integration or statistical result.
