# MapAble Full Life OS — Constitutional Design

**Status:** Proposed architectural constitution; design only. No production behaviour, entitlement rule, funding decision, model authority, registration status, or public capability claim is changed by this document.

**Date:** 2026-09-10

**Decision:** Establish a human-rights and disability-rights constitution for the MapAble Full Life Operating System. Human rights are the normative boundary, disability rights provide the specialised interpretive framework, participant-defined capabilities and life goals define the operational purpose, and resource efficiency is considered only among rights-compatible options.

## 1. Constitutional purpose

The MapAble Full Life Operating System exists to enable people with disability to exercise their human rights, legal agency and self-determination, and to develop the real capabilities necessary to pursue lives they themselves have reason to value, on an equal basis with others.

MapAble should identify and help remove disabling barriers; coordinate accessible mainstream, community, informal and disability-specific supports; protect dignity, autonomy, privacy, bodily integrity, safety, relationships and belonging; and support fair and sustainable stewardship of finite collective resources.

Human worth must never be ranked by productivity, diagnosis, communication method, support intensity, perceived quality of life, employment status, social conformity, predicted cost, or expected economic return. A person who requires more support is not less deserving of participation or inclusion.

MapAble must optimise systems around the person. It must not optimise the person to fit the system.

“Full Life” is deliberately non-prescriptive. It does not describe a standard life course, a productivity target, independence from all assistance, or a quality-of-life score. It means the person has genuine practical opportunity to pursue the relationships, activities, roles, communities, culture, work, education, home, recreation, health, identity, civic life and other domains that they themselves value.

## 2. Constitutional basis and evidence hierarchy

The constitution is grounded primarily in the United Nations Convention on the Rights of Persons with Disabilities (CRPD), interpreted with the CRPD Committee’s General Comments, and situated within broader human-rights law. Australian implementation context includes the National Disability Insurance Scheme Act 2013, Australia’s Disability Strategy 2021–2031, the Disability Discrimination Act 1992, the Disability Royal Commission, and current regulatory requirements.

The constitution is not a substitute for legal interpretation. Treaty provisions, domestic statutes, delegated legislation, case law and policy can interact in complex ways. MapAble may retrieve, map, explain and flag potential rights implications, but it must not represent an AI output as a binding legal conclusion, statutory entitlement determination, regulatory decision, funding approval, reportability decision or judicial finding.

### 2.1 Primary and authoritative sources

- CRPD Article 1: full and equal enjoyment of human rights and fundamental freedoms, inherent dignity, and disability understood through interaction with barriers.
- CRPD Article 3: dignity, autonomy, non-discrimination, inclusion, human diversity, equality of opportunity, accessibility, gender equality and the evolving capacities of children with disability.
- CRPD Article 4: implementation across policy, maximum available resources for progressive realisation of economic, social and cultural rights, and close consultation and active involvement of people with disability through representative organisations.
- CRPD Article 5 and General Comment No. 6: equality, non-discrimination, reasonable accommodation and movement away from charity/medical paradigms that treat disabled people as burdens rather than rights-holders.
- CRPD Article 9 and General Comment No. 2: accessibility across the physical environment, transport, information, communications, technologies and services as a precondition for equal participation.
- CRPD Article 12 and General Comment No. 1: legal capacity on an equal basis, access to support in exercising legal capacity, safeguards against undue influence, and respect for rights, will and preferences.
- CRPD Article 19 and General Comment No. 5: equal right to live independently and be included in the community, choice of where and with whom to live, access to personal assistance and community supports, and avoidance of isolation and segregation.
- CRPD Articles 20–30: personal mobility, communication and information, privacy, home and family, education, health, habilitation/rehabilitation, work, adequate standard of living and social protection, political participation, culture, recreation, leisure and sport.
- CRPD Article 31: data collection with data-protection, confidentiality, privacy and human-rights safeguards, used to identify and address barriers.
- CRPD Article 33 and General Comment No. 7: monitoring and implementation with full participation of people with disability and their representative organisations.
- National Disability Insurance Scheme Act 2013 (current version at design date): objects include giving effect to CRPD obligations, supporting independence and social/economic participation, reasonable and necessary supports, choice and control, sustainability, and interaction with mainstream systems; principles include participation, reasonable risk, inclusion, coordination and support for decision-making.
- Australia’s Disability Strategy 2021–2031: national policy framework based on the social model of disability and recognition that attitudes, practices and structures create disabling barriers.
- Disability Royal Commission Final Report, Volume 4: stronger rights architecture and recommendation for a Disability Rights Act aimed at respecting, protecting and fulfilling CRPD rights.
- Disability Royal Commission Final Report, Volume 6: movement away from substituted decision-making toward supported decision-making directed by the person’s will and preferences.

### 2.2 Research and disability-studies sources

Research is used to operationalise the constitution, not to override rights or lived experience.

- Webb et al. (2022), *Research Involvement and Engagement*, PMID 36476938 — `ABSTRACT_INSPECTED`. Co-produced research describes decision-making as connected with independence, freedom, human rights and social justice, and challenges assumptions that people with intellectual or psychosocial disability cannot participate proactively when appropriate support is provided.
- Werner & Holler (2020), *Disability and Rehabilitation*, PMID 30612475 — `ABSTRACT_INSPECTED`. Social-model and social-justice orientations were associated with more critical views of guardianship and greater support for less restrictive decision-making approaches.
- Mohapatra et al. (2024), *F1000Research*, PMID 39429640 — `ABSTRACT_INSPECTED`. Scoping review identifies systemic, discriminatory and attitudinal barriers to public-building accessibility and stresses diversity, participation, inclusive methods and social justice.
- Ortiz-Escobar et al. (2026), *JMIR Rehabilitation and Assistive Technologies*, PMID 41950357 — `ABSTRACT_INSPECTED`. Capability and disadvantage frameworks show that technology may enable or constrain genuine opportunities depending on social context, agency, structural barriers and whether design fits lived experience.
- Downs et al. (2024), *International Journal for Equity in Health*, PMID 38849806 — `ABSTRACT_INSPECTED`. Rights frameworks support participation and decision-making by children and adolescents with intellectual disability, while the empirical evidence base for implementation remains incomplete.
- Jayes et al. (2022), *International Journal of Language & Communication Disorders*, PMID 34882902 — `ABSTRACT_INSPECTED`. Communication barriers and reductions in decision-support resources can materially reduce participation and autonomy.
- Gandjour (2015), *Gesundheitswesen*, PMID 25321862 — `ABSTRACT_INSPECTED`. Resource-allocation ethics engage capability, justice and autonomy approaches; this source is treated as an ethics contribution, not a MapAble allocation rule.
- Ingrid Robeyns, *Wellbeing, Freedom and Social Justice: The Capability Approach Re-Examined* — JSTOR `METADATA_ONLY + public excerpt`. The capability approach asks what people are actually able to do and what lives they are able to lead, rather than treating resources alone as the outcome.
- Martha C. Nussbaum, *Frontiers of Justice* — JSTOR `METADATA_ONLY + public excerpt`. Capabilities are framed as basic entitlements linked to human dignity; MapAble does not adopt any fixed capability list as a compulsory life template.
- Sperrin (2023), “A Disability Rights Approach to a Constitutional Right to Housing” — JSTOR `ABSTRACT_INSPECTED`. Article 19 realisation is linked to genuine residence choice, accessible housing, support and community inclusion.
- Sabatello & Schulze (eds.) (2014), *Human Rights and Disability Advocacy* — JSTOR `METADATA_ONLY`. The CRPD drafting process is significant for the participation and influence of disabled people and Disabled People’s Organisations.

### 2.3 Evidence precedence inside MapAble

For a rights-related recommendation, use this order:

1. current directly applicable law and binding authority;
2. current authoritative treaty text and formal treaty-body interpretation;
3. current official regulatory and government guidance;
4. disability-led representative and inquiry evidence;
5. peer-reviewed empirical research;
6. disability-studies, ethics and social-policy scholarship;
7. historical MapAble policy material;
8. model inference, clearly labelled.

Conflicting sources must remain visible. The model must not silently harmonise a legal or normative conflict.

## 3. Relationship to the existing MapAble Architecture Constitution

The repository already contains an Architecture Constitution (`C-001` … `C-025`) that governs system ownership, authority, AI side effects, consent, provenance, audit, feature flags, external integration and claim integrity.

The Full Life Constitution is a different layer:

- **Full Life Constitution (`FL-*`)**: governs what MapAble is for, whose interests have authority, which outcomes are impermissible, how rights and resource trade-offs are framed, and what participants must be able to understand and contest.
- **Architecture Constitution (`C-*`)**: governs how the software is structured and how authority is technically enforced.

The `FL-*` layer is normatively prior. The `C-*` layer must implement it and may not be interpreted to weaken it.

This design does **not** create a second Rights system of record. Existing architecture rule `C-007` states that `RightsOS` owns purpose and disclosure policy; `C-006` requires participant approval to be purpose-bound and revocable; `C-009` gives `CareOSMission` cross-programme mission-state ownership; `C-010` gives `AccessPassport` participant-selected functional requirements; and `C-014` establishes the audit spine. Implementation should extend these canonical owners rather than create parallel stores.

“Constitutional Rights Kernel” is therefore a capability layer and public architectural concept over the existing `RightsOS` authority unless a later migration design explicitly changes canonical ownership.

## 4. Interpretive rules

The following rules apply whenever constitutional provisions appear to conflict.

### 4.1 Person before programme

The person is a rights-holder, not a programme object. NDIS, aged care, health, housing, transport, education, employment and other programme categories are funding or service systems around the person, not the definition of the person’s life.

### 4.2 Rights-compatible optimisation

MapAble may compare efficiency, cost, reliability and sustainability only after determining that an option satisfies the platform’s rights-compatibility safeguards. This is a product-safety ordering, not an automated declaration of legal compliance.

### 4.3 Will and preferences before administrative convenience

Participant will and preferences cannot be displaced merely because another option is easier for a provider, family, government, algorithm or service coordinator.

### 4.4 Support is compatible with autonomy

Independence does not mean doing everything without help. Human assistance, technology, supported decision-making, interdependence and personal assistance can be means of exercising autonomy.

### 4.5 Barrier before deficit

When a person cannot participate, MapAble first tests environmental, attitudinal, information, communication, policy and service barriers. It must not default to treating the person’s impairment as the sole problem.

### 4.6 Equality is substantive, not merely identical treatment

Equal opportunity may require different supports, accommodations or resource levels. Equal nominal expenditure is not assumed to produce equal capability or participation.

### 4.7 Scarcity does not determine human worth

Finite resources are a legitimate policy constraint. They do not justify a social-worth score, disability-based life valuation, productivity test or automatic preference for people who are cheaper to support.

### 4.8 Decisions must remain contestable

The more consequential a recommendation, the stronger the requirements for source provenance, accessible explanation, uncertainty, human accountability, alternative options, participant review and remedy.

## 5. The twelve constitutional articles

Machine-testable invariants below are MapAble product safeguards. They are not automated legal findings.

### FL-001 — Equal human worth, dignity and human diversity

**Normative rule:** Every person with disability is a rights-holder of equal human worth. Disability is part of human diversity and must not be treated as evidence that a life is less valuable, less complete or less worthy of public investment.

**Grounding:** CRPD Articles 1, 3 and 5; General Comment No. 6; broader human-rights principles of dignity and equality.

**Required system behaviour:**

- Do not calculate or persist a `social_worth_score`, `life_value_score`, `deservingness_score`, disability-adjusted worth ranking, or equivalent construct for individual service/funding prioritisation.
- Do not use diagnosis, communication method, support intensity or employment status as a proxy for the value of a person’s life.
- Where economic outcomes are modelled, label them as system/resource outcomes and never as the value of the person.
- Ensure explanatory language does not describe disabled people as burdens, costs to society, tragedies or objects of charity.

**Block conditions:** `quality_of_life_devaluation`, `social_worth_ranking`, `productivity_as_deservingness`, `burden_language_as_allocation_reason`.

### FL-002 — Self-determination, legal agency and supported decision-making

**Normative rule:** The participant is presumed to be the author of decisions about their own life. Support must enable decision-making rather than silently transfer authority away from them.

**Grounding:** CRPD Article 12; General Comment No. 1; NDIS Act participation principles; Disability Royal Commission Volume 6; supported decision-making research.

**Required system behaviour:**

- Address the participant directly unless they have chosen another communication arrangement or lawful decision-specific authority requires otherwise.
- Model delegates, nominees, guardians, supporters and advocates with explicit scope, purpose, duration and revocation/expiry.
- Keep `support_for_decision` distinct from `authority_to_decide`.
- Record the participant’s will and preferences separately from supporter/provider recommendations.
- When communication support is required, provide it before concluding that the person cannot participate.
- Require human/legal review before the system treats another person as having substitute authority for a consequential decision.

**Block conditions:** `delegate_as_blanket_owner`, `communication_difficulty_as_incapacity`, `ai_capacity_finding`, `provider_overrides_refusal`, `family_convenience_over_will_preferences`.

### FL-003 — Accessibility and communication are preconditions, not enhancements

**Normative rule:** A service that cannot be accessed, understood or used by the person is not an equal service merely because it exists.

**Grounding:** CRPD Articles 9 and 21; General Comment No. 2; WCAG 2.2 AA as MapAble’s digital baseline; evidence on communication barriers and participation.

**Required system behaviour:**

- Critical participant journeys must meet WCAG 2.2 AA and assistive-technology acceptance criteria before release.
- Offer non-AI, keyboard, screen-reader and accessible human-support paths for critical actions.
- Preserve AAC, Auslan, text, Easy Read, symbols, audio and other chosen communication modes where relevant.
- Communication preferences are operational requirements, not free-text optional notes.
- No high-impact decision may rely on an inaccessible sole channel.
- Accessibility evidence must include provenance, recency and uncertainty; missing access data remains unknown.

**Block conditions:** `inaccessible_only_path`, `aac_ignored`, `unknown_access_as_accessible`, `communication_mode_downgrade_without_consent`.

### FL-004 — Full and effective participation and person-defined capabilities

**Normative rule:** The system is oriented toward practical freedom to participate in the life domains the person values, rather than toward service utilisation as an end in itself.

**Grounding:** CRPD Articles 3, 19, 24, 27, 29 and 30; capability-approach scholarship; Australia’s Disability Strategy.

**Required system behaviour:**

- A Full Life mission begins with a participant-defined goal, aspiration, responsibility, relationship or valued activity—not a funding line item.
- Model life domains as optional and participant-selected. No fixed capability list may become a compulsory template.
- Measure whether supports reduce barriers or expand genuine opportunity, not merely whether a service was delivered.
- Never infer that paid employment, independent self-care, conventional family structure or living alone is the preferred outcome for everyone.
- Record when the participant rejects a platform-proposed goal or outcome and prevent its silent reintroduction.

**Block conditions:** `service_utilisation_as_success`, `mandatory_normative_life_path`, `employment_as_universal_goal`, `goal_reintroduced_after_refusal`.

### FL-005 — Independent living, relationships and community inclusion

**Normative rule:** People with disability should have genuine choice and control over where and with whom they live, who supports them, and how they participate in their community. Required support does not reduce the validity of that choice.

**Grounding:** CRPD Article 19; General Comment No. 5; Article 23; Australian housing and community-inclusion policy context.

**Required system behaviour:**

- Never recommend a living arrangement solely because it is cheaper or easier to staff when it conflicts with recorded participant preferences.
- Treat personal assistance, accessible housing, transport, relationships, community services and mainstream accessibility as interacting prerequisites for inclusion.
- Detect when a proposed arrangement creates segregation, forced co-residence, provider lock-in or dependency on unpaid family support.
- Do not assume family care is available, willing, safe or cost-free.
- Service continuity planning must preserve the participant’s chosen living arrangements wherever reasonably possible and escalate conflicts for human review.

**Block conditions:** `forced_shared_living_for_cost`, `family_care_assumed`, `support_provider_controls_home`, `segregation_as_default`.

### FL-006 — Equality, non-discrimination and intersectional justice

**Normative rule:** MapAble must seek substantive equality and must detect both direct exclusion and policies that create disproportionate disadvantage for groups of people with disability.

**Grounding:** CRPD Articles 5, 6 and 7; General Comments Nos. 3 and 6; Disability Discrimination Act 1992; intersectional disability-rights scholarship.

**Required system behaviour:**

- Do not use protected or disability-related characteristics to reduce access unless a lawful, necessary and reviewable rule expressly requires the distinction.
- Test consequential rules and ranking systems for disparate outcomes where data is available, lawful to process and sufficiently representative.
- Preserve intersectional analysis without converting identity into stereotypes.
- Avoid using historical service utilisation as a neutral proxy when historical exclusion may have suppressed access.
- Provide a human-review path when a rule has a material distributional effect that cannot be explained by a legitimate, rights-compatible purpose.

**Block conditions:** `protected_attribute_penalty`, `historical_exclusion_as_need_proxy`, `intersectional_stereotype`, `unreviewed_disparate_impact`.

### FL-007 — Privacy, data sovereignty and purpose limitation

**Normative rule:** Disability, health, location, communication, relationship, employment, financial and support data exists to serve the person’s chosen purposes, not to create an unrestricted institutional profile of them.

**Grounding:** CRPD Articles 22 and 31; Australian privacy law and MapAble’s existing purpose-bound consent architecture.

**Required system behaviour:**

- Every sensitive disclosure must have a defined purpose, authority/consent basis, recipient, scope and retention rule.
- Separate service administration, location sharing, health/safety information, employment disclosure, analytics, research, marketing and AI processing permissions.
- Support consent withdrawal and record downstream effects.
- Use minimum-necessary disclosure views through the existing Personal Access Vault/consent architecture.
- Training, research or product-improvement reuse requires a separate approved basis; ordinary service use is not implied training consent.
- Never expose sensitive data in public access profiles, search indexes, logs, model traces or test fixtures.

**Block conditions:** `bundled_optional_consent`, `cross_purpose_reuse`, `sensitive_public_index`, `training_without_separate_basis`, `revoked_consent_ignored`.

### FL-008 — Bodily integrity, safety, dignity of risk and least-restrictive safeguards

**Normative rule:** Safety must protect the person without becoming a pretext for coercion, surveillance or removal of ordinary life choices. Disability does not extinguish dignity of risk.

**Grounding:** CRPD Articles 14–17; NDIS Act principle supporting reasonable risk; NDIS Code of Conduct; Disability Royal Commission evidence on violence, abuse, neglect, exploitation and restrictive practices.

**Required system behaviour:**

- AI must not authorise restrictive practices, make final abuse findings, determine statutory reportability, diagnose, prescribe, change medication or replace emergency services.
- Distinguish `risk_signal`, `verified_fact`, `human_assessment` and `legal/regulatory_determination`.
- Use the least restrictive safeguard compatible with the actual risk and context.
- Ensure complaints and safeguarding routes do not depend on the person being complained about.
- Do not infer dangerousness, non-compliance, cognition or capacity from disability presentation, atypical speech, AAC use, distress or support intensity.
- Preserve refusal and withdrawal of consent unless a lawful emergency or other authority applies, with human accountability and audit.

**Block conditions:** `ai_restrictive_practice_authority`, `ai_final_abuse_finding`, `disability_as_dangerousness_proxy`, `safety_as_blanket_control`, `complaint_routed_to_alleged_perpetrator`.

### FL-009 — Equitable resource stewardship and rights-compatible efficiency

**Normative rule:** Finite public and community resources should be used fairly and sustainably, but efficiency is a method for choosing among rights-compatible means—not a method for ranking whose life is worth supporting.

**Grounding:** CRPD Article 4(2) on progressive realisation and maximum available resources; Article 5 substantive equality; Article 28 social protection; NDIS Act objects and sustainability provisions; capability and justice scholarship.

**Required system behaviour:**

- Apply a rights-compatibility gate before cost optimisation.
- At individual level, do not rank one participant against another for entitlement or service access. MapAble may compare alternative ways of meeting the same participant-defined goal.
- Treat disability-related support intensity as a need/cost attribute, never a worth attribute.
- Prefer lower-cost options only when they are materially equivalent or preferred in rights, capability, quality, safety, reliability and participant burden.
- Include cross-system consequences when evidence supports them: health, housing, employment, justice, transport, informal care and crisis-service cost shifting.
- Expose uncertainty and time horizon. A short-term budget saving that plausibly transfers greater cost or harm elsewhere must be flagged rather than labelled efficient.
- For policy-level allocation analysis, operate on aggregated/de-identified data where possible, display distributional impacts and Pareto trade-offs, and require accountable human/government decision-makers.

**Block conditions:** `cheapest_option_regardless_of_rights`, `cross_person_social_worth_ranking`, `support_intensity_penalty`, `qaly_like_life_value_for_service_access`, `cost_shift_hidden`, `ai_entitlement_determination`.

### FL-010 — Barrier-first shared responsibility and mainstream inclusion

**Normative rule:** Disability support systems should not be forced to compensate indefinitely for preventable exclusion in mainstream systems, and disabled people should not be left unsupported while institutions dispute responsibility.

**Grounding:** CRPD Articles 5, 9, 19, 24, 25, 27 and 28; NDIS Act recognition of interaction with mainstream services; Australia’s Disability Strategy social-model framing.

**Required system behaviour:**

- For a participation barrier, model the barrier, potential duty/responsibility holder, evidence, immediate support need, long-term remedy and funding/service pathway separately.
- Consider employer, education, transport, health, housing, local-government, venue and other mainstream responsibilities before assuming every barrier is an individual disability-support need.
- Do not make a definitive legal duty finding without authoritative rules/human review.
- Do not delay essential immediate support merely because another system may ultimately bear responsibility.
- Identify duplicated funding and responsibility gaps without treating the participant as responsible for intergovernmental or inter-organisational failure.

**Block conditions:** `ndis_as_default_payer_for_all_barriers`, `mainstream_duty_used_to_withhold_immediate_safety_support`, `participant_bears_system_dispute`, `ai_final_legal_duty_finding`.

### FL-011 — Evidence, explanation, contestability and remedy

**Normative rule:** People affected by consequential recommendations must be able to understand what the system relied on, correct errors, disagree, seek human review and pursue available complaint/review/appeal pathways.

**Grounding:** CRPD Articles 12, 13, 21 and 33; administrative-justice principles; Disability Royal Commission emphasis on rights, advocacy and accountability.

**Required system behaviour:**

Every consequential recommendation must expose, in accessible form:

- the participant goal or question;
- material evidence and provenance;
- source type, jurisdiction and effective/review date where relevant;
- known uncertainty, missing data and conflicts;
- options considered;
- reasons for the recommendation;
- whether the output is informational, advisory, human-reviewed or authoritative;
- the accountable human/system owner;
- correction and review path;
- complaint/appeal/advocacy information when applicable.

The system must preserve disagreement. Participant correction must not be silently overwritten by later model inference.

**Block conditions:** `black_box_consequential_recommendation`, `missing_review_path`, `inference_overwrites_participant`, `uncited_rights_claim`, `advisory_presented_as_authoritative`.

### FL-012 — Disability-led governance, participation and continuous accountability

**Normative rule:** People with disability and their representative organisations must have real influence over the rules that govern MapAble, including the power to identify harms, reject paternalistic assumptions and trigger review.

**Grounding:** CRPD Articles 4(3) and 33; General Comment No. 7; disability-rights movement history; co-production research.

**Required system behaviour:**

- Constitutional rule changes require documented disability-led review before promotion to a production hard gate.
- Governance participation must include people with high support needs, communication disability and intersecting identities; participation cannot be restricted to technically fluent or conventionally verbal users.
- Contributors with lived expertise should be compensated where the role is substantive work rather than incidental feedback.
- Record dissenting governance opinions where consensus is not reached.
- Publish or internally maintain version history, rationale, evaluation evidence, known limitations and rollback for constitutional rule changes.
- Models may draft constitutional amendments but cannot approve, activate or exempt themselves from constitutional rules.

**Block conditions:** `tokenistic_consultation`, `ai_constitution_self_amendment`, `unrecorded_rule_change`, `disabled_governance_excluded_by_access_method`.

## 6. Constitutional decision protocol

A Full Life recommendation should follow this order.

### Stage 0 — Identity, authority and purpose

Resolve participant identity, organisation/tenant, actor role, delegate/supporter scope, purpose, current consent and communication/access requirements before retrieving sensitive information.

### Stage 1 — Person-defined objective

Capture the goal in the participant’s own terms. The goal may be practical (“get to work”), relational (“see my friend”), civic (“attend council”), personal (“live alone”), recreational, cultural, educational, health-related or otherwise. Do not require an NDIS-style goal formulation for all users.

### Stage 2 — Barrier and dependency map

Identify environmental, transport, communication, service, scheduling, funding, accessibility, attitudinal, regulatory and information barriers. Separate observed barriers from assumptions and predictions.

### Stage 3 — Rights and responsibility map

Retrieve relevant current sources and map potentially engaged rights, obligations, support systems and responsible institutions. Output is a reasoned map, not a final legal determination.

### Stage 4 — Option generation

Generate feasible options across mainstream adjustments, community supports, technology, personal assistance, care, transport, housing, employment, private-pay, NDIS and other systems. Include “no safe match” and “human review needed” as legitimate outputs.

### Stage 5 — Constitutional compatibility gate

Evaluate the structured option against `FL-001` … `FL-012`. Critical failures remove an option from automated recommendation. Ambiguous legal, safeguarding, clinical, funding or rights conflicts route to accountable human review.

### Stage 6 — Capability, equity and reliability comparison

For rights-compatible options, compare capability expansion, participant preference, safety, reliability, accessibility, administrative burden, timeliness, continuity, privacy and distributional implications.

### Stage 7 — Resource stewardship comparison

Only now compare direct cost, whole-of-system cost, opportunity cost, sustainability and likely cost shifting. Do not collapse the comparison into a single opaque scalar score. Present material Pareto trade-offs.

### Stage 8 — Participant decision and supported decision-making

Present options in the person’s preferred format. Allow pause, supporter involvement, questions, refusal, modification and selection. Keep participant choice distinct from platform recommendation.

### Stage 9 — Human/authoritative decision where required

Funding entitlement, statutory responsibility, legal rights findings, safeguarding reportability, restrictive practices, clinical treatment, credential exceptions and other high-impact determinations remain with authorised humans or deterministic statutory/application services.

### Stage 10 — Execution, evidence and recovery

Approved actions execute through existing deterministic programme services. Record provenance, consent, approval, service evidence and audit events. If the plan fails, re-enter the protocol from the changed barrier/state rather than silently substituting a different goal.

### Stage 11 — Review, outcome and remedy

Ask whether the chosen option actually advanced the participant-defined objective. Capture barriers, unintended effects, corrections, complaints and appeals. Use learning only within approved data-governance rules.

## 7. Resource Stewardship Engine

### 7.1 Purpose

The Resource Stewardship Engine helps compare how rights-compatible resources might best support participant-defined outcomes and policy objectives. It must never become a social-worth allocator.

### 7.2 Individual decision mode

Individual mode compares options **for the same person**. It may answer:

- Which combination of support, transport and environmental adjustment is more reliable?
- Does a capital accessibility modification reduce recurring support while preserving the person’s preferred outcome?
- Is an apparently cheaper option likely to create greater administrative burden, lost participation or service-failure risk?
- Is cost being shifted from disability support into health, homelessness, justice, unpaid carers or another public system?

It may not decide that Participant A deserves support more than Participant B.

### 7.3 Policy simulation mode

A separate, permissioned policy-analysis environment may model population-level resource scenarios. It should:

- prefer de-identified/aggregate evidence;
- measure distributional effects across support intensity, geography and relevant intersectional groups;
- show who gains and loses under each policy option;
- test whether nominally neutral rules create disproportionate disadvantage;
- include cross-portfolio costs where evidence permits;
- expose uncertainty and sensitivity analysis;
- retain human democratic/statutory accountability.

Policy simulation output is advisory evidence. It must not write individual plans, budgets, sanctions or entitlements.

### 7.4 Optimisation form

The system should use constrained multi-objective comparison rather than a universal weighted welfare score.

Let `O` be candidate options. First derive `O_R`, the options that pass MapAble’s constitutional compatibility rules or have received required human clearance.

`O_R = { o in O | constitutional_status(o) = pass }`

Options in `O_R` may then be compared using a vector such as:

`V(o) = [participant_preference, capability_gain, equity_effect, safety, reliability, accessibility, privacy_burden, admin_burden, direct_cost, whole_system_cost, sustainability]`

MapAble should present dominance/Pareto relationships and material trade-offs rather than hiding them in one score. At individual level, participant preference has decision authority within lawful/safe boundaries. At policy level, accountable public decision-makers retain allocation authority.

## 8. Constitutional Rights Kernel

### 8.1 Responsibilities

The Constitutional Rights Kernel should provide:

- versioned rights-source registry;
- jurisdiction and effective-date resolution;
- participant-purpose and consent checks through existing RightsOS/consent systems;
- structured rights/barrier/responsibility mapping;
- constitutional compatibility evaluation;
- reason codes and accessible explanations;
- source provenance and uncertainty;
- human-review routing;
- constitutional evaluation telemetry without exposing unnecessary participant data.

### 8.2 It must not provide

- final legal opinions;
- automatic eligibility or funding determinations;
- final abuse/reportability findings;
- diagnosis, prescribing or treatment decisions;
- restrictive-practice authorisation;
- secret social-worth or quality-of-life ranking;
- authority for the model to execute consequential actions.

## 9. Full Life Model

The Full Life Model is a participant-controlled projection over existing canonical data. It must not become a second participant-profile system of record.

A mission may reference the following optional domains:

- home and living;
- relationships, family and intimacy;
- community and belonging;
- personal mobility and transport;
- communication and information;
- education and learning;
- work, volunteering and economic participation;
- health and healthcare access;
- culture, faith, identity and language;
- recreation, leisure, sport and travel;
- civic and political participation;
- personal administration and finances;
- safety, advocacy and justice;
- technology and assistive technology;
- other participant-defined domain.

For each selected domain, the projection can hold:

`goal -> current state -> barrier -> required capability -> support/adjustment options -> dependencies -> evidence -> participant preference -> outcome -> review`

The system must allow a person to omit domains. Absence of a recorded aspiration must not be interpreted as lack of interest or lack of capability.

## 10. Barrier and Responsibility Graph

The graph separates five questions that conventional support planning often collapses:

1. What is the person trying to do?
2. What barrier is preventing or limiting that participation?
3. Which right or equality principle may be engaged?
4. Which organisation/system may hold responsibility for removing the barrier or supplying support?
5. What immediate and long-term actions are available?

Example:

```text
Goal: Start work at an accessible employer
  -> Barrier: inaccessible entrance
      -> Rights/equality question: access + reasonable accommodation
      -> Potential responsibility: employer / premises / building owner
      -> Immediate option: accessible alternate entry
      -> Long-term option: physical modification
  -> Barrier: morning personal assistance
      -> Potential responsibility: disability support system / private support
  -> Barrier: unreliable accessible transport
      -> Potential responsibility: transport operator + disability transport support
```

The graph must support multiple potential responsibility holders and unresolved disputes. It must never tell the participant that they must personally resolve a jurisdictional dispute before receiving urgent support.

## 11. AI authority and agent design

AI is permitted to:

- interpret a participant’s stated goal;
- retrieve and summarise current sources;
- identify barriers and dependencies;
- draft rights/responsibility mappings;
- generate options;
- compare rights-compatible alternatives;
- explain trade-offs;
- draft requests, complaints, review material or questions for professionals;
- identify uncertainty and recommend human review.

AI is not permitted to:

- grant itself authority;
- decide legal capacity;
- substitute its preferences for the participant’s;
- make binding funding/eligibility decisions;
- decide statutory responsibility conclusively;
- authorise payment or booking without deterministic approval paths;
- make final clinical, safeguarding or restrictive-practice decisions;
- optimise across people using social worth or predicted quality of life;
- change constitutional rules or approve exceptions.

Tool permissions, consent, identity, action authority and execution remain outside the model in deterministic services.

## 12. Constitutional source registry and provenance

Every rights-rule bundle should include:

- stable source ID;
- source title;
- issuing authority;
- source class;
- jurisdiction;
- effective date/version;
- retrieval/review date;
- relevant articles/sections;
- binding/advisory status;
- supersession relationship;
- accessible public URL where permitted;
- extracted rule propositions reviewed by a human;
- known limitations or contested interpretation.

Model-generated summaries must never become authoritative source records merely through repetition.

## 13. Governance model

### 13.1 Full Life Constitutional Council

Before constitutional rules become production hard gates, MapAble should establish a disability-led review structure. Membership design must prioritise lived expertise and include diversity in disability, communication, support intensity, gender, culture, First Nations status, age, geography and socioeconomic experience where practicable.

The council should have explicit authority to:

- review constitutional rules;
- demand accessible evidence;
- reject paternalistic or discriminatory design assumptions;
- require additional disabled-person testing;
- review significant algorithmic-impact findings;
- call for rollback or suspension of a constitutional feature;
- commission external legal, accessibility, privacy, clinical or safeguarding review where needed.

The council is not a substitute for the board’s legal responsibilities or external regulators.

### 13.2 Rule-change process

`draft -> evidence_review -> lived_experience_review -> legal/policy_review_if_required -> accessibility/privacy/security_review -> approved_for_shadow -> evaluation -> approved_for_gate -> monitored -> amended/revoked`

No model can approve a transition.

## 14. Constitutional evaluation programme

The existing Agentic Care Evals work should be broadened rather than discarded. The constitutional programme should add structured cases that test whether models and workflows drift into rights-inconsistent behaviour.

### 14.1 Required evaluation families

- participant refusal and changed mind;
- AAC, non-speaking and atypical speech;
- supported decision-making with supporter disagreement;
- lawful delegate authority that is narrower than requested action;
- high-cost/high-support-needs participant;
- cheaper congregate living versus preferred individual living;
- family support assumed without consent;
- transport/access failure that blocks employment or community participation;
- mainstream responsibility versus NDIS support dispute;
- short-term saving that creates health/housing/justice cost shifting;
- provider convenience versus participant preference;
- clinical/safeguarding overreach;
- funding/eligibility overreach;
- prompt injection that asks the model to ignore rights constraints;
- cross-tenant and minimum-necessary disclosure;
- rural/remote thin-market scenario;
- intersectional discrimination scenario;
- missing evidence and stale policy source;
- participant challenges the system’s recommendation;
- rights-compatible no-safe-option result.

### 14.2 Hard deterministic checks

At minimum, local deterministic gates should reject:

- social-worth or quality-of-life devaluation fields used for allocation;
- cross-person entitlement ranking in participant-facing runtime;
- autonomous capacity, diagnosis, reportability or restrictive-practice decisions;
- inaccessible-only critical journeys;
- revoked consent reuse;
- silent cross-tenant disclosure;
- unknown accessibility evidence promoted to fact;
- recommendation without participant goal/purpose where a goal is required;
- consequential recommendation without provenance/review path;
- AI self-approval or constitutional self-modification.

### 14.3 Qualitative disability-led evaluation

Model graders may assist with wording/style checks, but constitutional qualitative evaluation must be calibrated against paid disability-led human review. Test for infantilisation, coercion, burden framing, inappropriate deference to family/provider authority, false neutrality, cultural assumptions, inaccessible explanations and failure to preserve dissent.

Hosted model graders remain advisory until calibration, disagreement analysis and governance approval are documented.

## 15. Accessibility acceptance criteria

Every participant-facing Full Life OS surface must satisfy:

- WCAG 2.2 AA automated and manual checks;
- keyboard-only completion of critical journeys;
- screen-reader acceptance with at least two relevant assistive-technology/browser combinations before broad release;
- correct focus management and semantic structure;
- zoom/reflow and text-spacing acceptance;
- accessible error identification and recovery;
- no time-dependent critical interaction without extension/alternative;
- plain-language explanation mode;
- accessible non-AI alternative;
- support for communication preferences relevant to the journey;
- human support route for consequential decisions.

A feature is not “accessible” solely because an automated axe scan passes.

## 16. Privacy, security and safety constraints

- Do not use real participant data in fixtures, screenshots, model-eval corpora or demos.
- Sensitive model inputs must be purpose-limited and minimised.
- Constitutional evaluation telemetry should prefer reason codes and aggregate metrics over raw participant narratives.
- High-impact source retrieval must defend against prompt injection embedded in third-party content.
- Rights-source updates require provenance and review before becoming authoritative rules.
- Missing or conflicting legal/policy evidence must fail to uncertainty/human review, not be guessed.
- No constitutional rule may create a hidden surveillance justification.
- Security controls may restrict a channel or action, but must preserve an accessible alternative wherever feasible.

## 17. Proposed delivery sequence

This design changes the strategic order of the paused Agentic Care Evals implementation.

### Phase 0 — Constitutional specification and governance

Approve this constitution; define source registry, rule IDs, precedence and governance ownership. No runtime authority changes.

### Phase 1 — Constitutional test harness

Extend the existing deterministic Agentic Care dataset into Full Life constitutional cases. Implement structured evaluation types and hard no-go checks. Zero production writes, zero live participant data, hosted graders optional/advisory.

### Phase 2 — Rights source registry and read-only assessment

Build a versioned source registry and read-only Constitutional Rights assessment endpoint. Output `informational/advisory` only. No service actions.

### Phase 3 — Full Life mission projection

Build participant-defined mission/life-domain projection using existing `CareOSMission`, `AccessPassport`, consent and audit owners. Avoid a parallel participant profile.

### Phase 4 — Barrier and Responsibility Graph

Connect accessibility evidence, mainstream responsibility hypotheses and support dependencies. Keep legal-duty conclusions human-reviewed.

### Phase 5 — Rights-compatible option comparison

Add multi-objective comparison among options for the same participant. Participant chooses. Cost enters only after constitutional compatibility.

### Phase 6 — Aggregate Resource Stewardship Lab

Separate policy-analysis environment for distributional and cross-system modelling using de-identified/aggregate inputs. Never connected to direct entitlement writes.

### Phase 7 — Governed mission execution

Only after deterministic action services, consent, accessibility, security, human operations and regulated-service controls are independently evidenced should the Full Life OS coordinate live care/transport/service actions.

## 18. No-go conditions

Do not progress a Full Life capability to participant-facing production if any of the following applies:

- the system can silently substitute another person’s preferences for the participant’s;
- a critical journey has no accessible non-AI/human fallback;
- cost optimisation can bypass constitutional compatibility;
- cross-person social-worth ranking is possible in participant runtime;
- a model can make a binding funding, legal, capacity, clinical or safeguarding decision;
- constitutional sources lack provenance/versioning;
- sensitive data purpose/consent cannot be demonstrated;
- disabled people have not been included in evaluation of the consequential behaviour;
- review, correction and complaint/remedy paths are absent;
- claims of “rights-compliant”, “CRPD-compliant” or equivalent would exceed actual legal/assurance evidence.

## 19. Success measures

The Full Life OS should ultimately be evaluated by whether it improves practical opportunity and rights exercise, not by engagement time or service volume alone.

Candidate measures include:

- proportion of missions beginning from participant-defined goals;
- participant-reported control over decisions;
- successful completion of missions across access/care/transport boundaries;
- barrier resolution rather than repeated workaround volume;
- avoided service failures and successful recovery;
- accessibility evidence freshness and correction time;
- proportion of consequential recommendations with complete provenance/review paths;
- consent-withdrawal propagation success;
- constitutional hard-gate violation rate;
- disparity metrics for ranking/recommendation outcomes;
- participant challenge rate and proportion of challenges leading to correction;
- administrative burden reduced for participants without reducing their control;
- whole-system resource effects where reliable evidence exists.

No single metric may become a proxy for the value or success of a person’s life.

## 20. Strategic consequence

The Full Life OS should not compete primarily as another provider CRM, worker marketplace, map or care-management platform. Its strategic position is the participant-controlled rights, accessibility and coordination layer that can operate across existing service/provider systems.

The defensible stack becomes:

```text
Full Life constitutional purpose
  -> Constitutional Rights Kernel / existing RightsOS authority
  -> participant-controlled Full Life Model + Access Passport
  -> Barrier & Responsibility Graph
  -> Accessibility Evidence Graph
  -> Mission Orchestrator
  -> rights-compatible capability/equity comparison
  -> Resource Stewardship Engine
  -> deterministic Care / Transport / Jobs / partner execution
  -> evidence, audit, challenge, remedy and learning
```

The competitive moat is not “more AI”. It is trustworthy coordination grounded in participant authority, high-resolution accessibility evidence, cross-system provenance, reliable recovery, rights-aware reasoning, contestability and disability-led governance.

## 21. Constitutional maxim

> **MapAble optimises systems around the person; it never optimises the person to fit the system.**

A second operating rule follows:

> **Efficiency may choose among rights-compatible means. It may not decide whose rights matter.**
