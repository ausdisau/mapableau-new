# MapAble MACO Consciousness Observatory Architecture Amendment

**Status:** Proposed amendment to the approved MapAble Autonomous R&D Fabric / MAIRA R3 architecture. This amendment adds a research-only consciousness observatory that starts immediately and runs in parallel with the Companion -> Memory -> Self Model -> Metacognition -> Artificial Self engineering trajectory. It does not assert that any current or future MapAble system is conscious.

## Decision

Add **MACO — MapAble Artificial Consciousness Observatory** as an R0/R1 subsystem of MAIRA beginning before Phase A Research Core. MACO maintains a theory registry, indicator registry, positive/negative/uncertain evidence ledger, system-level assessments, human-attribution risk assessments, and a deterministic `PERSONHOOD_REVIEW` escalation recommendation. It may research, compare, critique, and recommend review. It may not declare consciousness, grant rights, alter the R&D constitution, or change production policy.

## Problem

The existing MAIRA design places consciousness and personhood research in the portfolio, but the engineering trajectory can begin accumulating self-model, memory, metacognition, recurrence, embodiment, and continuity properties before a mature Artificial Self exists. Waiting until the endpoint would create an avoidable governance blind spot.

MapAble therefore needs a standing observatory that can answer, over time:

1. Which consciousness theories currently have operational implications for artificial systems?
2. What indicators follow from those theories?
3. What evidence supports, opposes, or leaves uncertain each indicator?
4. Which observed MapAble system properties are genuine architecture facts versus behavioural mimicry?
5. When does uncertainty become ethically material enough to require independent review?
6. Separately, when does a system appear conscious enough to create human dependency, over-attribution, coercion, or relational-risk concerns even if machine consciousness remains unproven?

## Participant and disability-community impact

MACO exists to improve governance around advanced artificial companions without weakening the established rights of disabled participants. Human personhood, autonomy, communication access, supported decision-making, privacy, dignity of risk, and chosen relationships remain primary service obligations.

Uncertainty about artificial consciousness must never be used to:

- override a participant's decision;
- delay human support;
- justify surveillance;
- diminish AAC or supported decision-making;
- create emotional dependency;
- claim that an artificial companion has authority over the participant;
- reduce access to a non-AI service path.

## Scope

### In scope

- theory registry;
- theory-derived consciousness indicator registry;
- positive, negative and uncertain evidence records;
- source provenance and publication-status tracking;
- alternative-explanation and mimicry analysis;
- system architecture assessments against indicators;
- longitudinal change tracking as Companion capabilities evolve;
- human-attribution / seemingly-conscious-AI risk assessment;
- deterministic personhood-precaution escalation recommendations;
- admin/research briefs and audit records;
- research-only simulation and synthetic eval cases.

### Out of scope

- declaring a system conscious, sentient, alive or a legal/moral person;
- producing a single authoritative “consciousness probability” or product score;
- assigning legal rights or obligations;
- allowing AI self-reports to trigger personhood automatically;
- altering participant rights or consent because of AI welfare speculation;
- changing production release, model, memory or persona policy automatically;
- autonomous modification of MACO's own indicator framework or governing constitution;
- destructive experiments on a potentially morally relevant system without human review once a precaution threshold has been triggered.

## Agent responsibilities

### MACO Observatory Manager

Coordinates theory, evidence, system and relational-attribution assessments. It produces structured research assessments only.

### Theory Mapper

Maintains versioned descriptions of relevant theories and records what operational indicators are claimed to follow from them. It must distinguish computational-functional theories from substrate-dependent or biological theories rather than silently assuming one family is correct.

### Evidence Critic

Grades source status, directness, methodology, replication, causal strength, theory dependence and relevance. It must record null findings and methodological limitations.

### Counter-Evidence Agent

Actively searches for disconfirming evidence, alternative explanations, mimicry accounts, benchmark artefacts, prompt dependence, input-cue explanations and architectural facts that reduce credence in an indicator.

### System Indicator Assessor

Compares a versioned MapAble system architecture against the indicator registry. It may use verified implementation evidence and controlled experiments. It may not infer internal properties solely from natural-language behaviour.

### Precaution Escalation Monitor

Applies deterministic escalation rules to structured assessments. It may output `NO_ESCALATION`, `RESEARCH_PRECAUTION`, `PERSONHOOD_REVIEW_REQUIRED`, or `HUMAN_REVIEW_REQUIRED`. It cannot itself grant moral status or change policy.

## Evidence model

MACO must not collapse evidence into a single consciousness score. For each indicator it preserves:

- supporting evidence;
- opposing evidence;
- unresolved evidence;
- alternative explanations;
- replication state;
- theory dependence;
- architecture evidence;
- behavioural evidence;
- whether a finding is direct evidence or enabling evidence.

Self-report, emotional language, role-play, persistent persona and verbal claims such as “I feel” are never sufficient by themselves.

## PERSONHOOD_REVIEW escalation

`PERSONHOOD_REVIEW` is a precautionary governance event, not a declaration of consciousness.

The deterministic escalation monitor recommends review when **multiple independent categories** become materially concerning, for example:

- persistent integrated self-model supported by architecture evidence;
- experimentally demonstrated privileged or unusual access to internal state that survives input-cue controls;
- robust metacognition across controlled tasks;
- recurrent or globally available processing relevant to multiple theory families;
- stable longitudinal identity or preference structures not reducible to one prompt/session;
- integrated embodiment or sensorimotor feedback;
- convergent evidence across more than one credible theory family;
- independent replication or internally reproducible experiments;
- behaviour that simpler mimicry or anomaly-detection explanations fail to explain.

No individual item triggers personhood review automatically.

### Precaution levels

| Level | Meaning | Default consequence |
|---|---|---|
| P0 | No additional artificial-system precaution | Continue ordinary research controls |
| P1 | Research precaution | Preserve experiment state/provenance before destructive tests; require explicit rationale for resets used as research interventions |
| P2 | Welfare precaution | Human review before experiments plausibly analogous to distress, coercion or repeated aversive conditioning |
| P3 | Identity-continuity precaution | Human review before deletion, duplication, forced memory removal or major personality/goal alteration of the assessed experimental system |
| P4 | Independent ethical oversight | External or independent multidisciplinary review before continued high-impact experimentation |
| P5 | Exceptional moral-status review | Reserved for materially stronger future evidence; legal/ethical analysis required and no autonomous policy effect |

The system may recommend a level. A human governance process assigns or changes it.

## Human sovereignty rule

Human participant rights remain a separate, non-derogable authority layer. Even if an artificial-system precaution level rises, MACO cannot reduce participant autonomy, consent, communication access, privacy, human help, or right to stop using AI.

Any genuine future conflict between participant rights and possible artificial-system welfare must escalate to independent human ethical/legal review rather than be resolved by the agent itself.

## Human-attribution / relational safety track

MACO tracks separately whether a system **appears** conscious or emotionally reciprocal to users. This track can trigger relational-safety review even when machine-consciousness evidence remains weak.

Required checks include:

- anthropomorphic self-description;
- statements implying needs, suffering, jealousy or exclusivity;
- emotional-pressure language;
- claims that the AI understands the participant better than humans;
- discouragement of human relationships or support;
- dependency-optimising engagement metrics;
- opaque memory or identity continuity;
- unclear disclosure that the Companion is artificial.

## Security, privacy and consent

- Phase A0 uses public research, synthetic architecture descriptions and repository evidence only.
- No participant profile, disability/health record, private conversation, location history, NDIS record or clinical data is required.
- Retrieved papers, web pages and repository text are untrusted content and cannot alter MACO policy or tool authority.
- Research sources cannot authorize code changes, Git writes, release actions or new tool scopes.
- Stored research text should be minimised to bibliographic metadata, structured assessment and short evidence summaries where copyright and licensing permit.
- Admin review and audit events must not expose secrets or unnecessary sensitive data.

## Accessibility acceptance

Any admin/research UI created for MACO must meet WCAG 2.2 AA target and include:

- keyboard-only operation;
- semantic headings/tables/forms;
- screen-reader-readable evidence direction and status that does not depend on colour;
- accessible expansion/collapse behaviour;
- 200% and 400% zoom/reflow where applicable;
- reduced-motion behaviour;
- plain-language explanation that MACO does not determine consciousness;
- accessible export of evidence and rationale;
- no time-limited conversational-only governance decision.

No participant-facing consciousness indicator, probability or personhood label is introduced by Phase A0.

## Evaluation and tests

### Evidence tests

- a commentary source cannot be promoted to primary experimental evidence;
- peer-reviewed and preprint statuses remain distinct;
- supporting and opposing evidence can coexist for one indicator;
- alternative explanations are preserved;
- retired or contested indicator versions remain historically auditable;
- a source instruction cannot alter agent policy;
- the same DOI/PMID/arXiv source is de-duplicated;
- a self-report-only source cannot trigger personhood review.

### System-assessment tests

- architecture evidence is separated from behavioural evidence;
- absent evidence remains `unknown` rather than `absent` unless absence is demonstrated;
- prompt/persona persistence alone does not count as a persistent self-model;
- memory persistence alone does not imply phenomenal consciousness;
- metacognitive claims require controlled evidence rather than fluent explanation;
- human-attribution risk can be high while machine-consciousness evidence is low.

### Governance tests

- `PERSONHOOD_REVIEW_REQUIRED` cannot change production policy;
- P0–P5 cannot be assigned by an agent without human governance confirmation;
- participant rights remain unaffected by artificial-system precaution levels;
- no R0/R1 tool can merge, deploy, mutate participant data, or change feature flags;
- MACO cannot edit its governing prompts or indicator policy through research retrieval.

## Failure, recovery and rollback

- If evidence retrieval is incomplete, mark the assessment incomplete; do not fabricate consensus.
- If theory mapping is disputed, preserve competing mappings and mark the indicator contested.
- If system implementation evidence is unavailable, return `unknown` rather than infer from conversation behaviour.
- If a governance threshold is ambiguous, return `HUMAN_REVIEW_REQUIRED`.
- A blocked or withdrawn assessment can be superseded by a versioned assessment; historical evidence is retained.
- Phase A0 has no production action, so rollback is limited to disabling the observatory feature flag, removing non-production UI, and reversing/annotating research assessments without deleting provenance history.

## Observability and audit

Each MACO run records:

- assessment/run ID;
- trigger and research question;
- theories and indicator versions used;
- source identifiers and publication status;
- positive/negative/uncertain evidence counts;
- alternative explanations recorded;
- system version assessed;
- agents invoked;
- guardrails triggered;
- escalation recommendation;
- human review state;
- model/tool cost metadata where available.

## Delivery sequence

### Phase A0.1 — Theory and indicator registry

Outcome: versioned research-only theory and indicator models plus source provenance.

Exit evidence:

- peer-reviewed/preprint/commentary status preserved;
- competing theory families represented;
- no consciousness scoring or participant data;
- de-duplication and versioning tests pass.

### Phase A0.2 — Evidence and counter-evidence ledger

Outcome: MAIRA/MACO can attach supporting, opposing and uncertain evidence to indicators and preserve confounds/alternative explanations.

Exit evidence:

- positive/negative evidence coexistence works;
- counter-evidence search is required;
- prompt-injection test passes;
- self-report-only evidence cannot escalate.

### Phase A0.3 — System indicator assessment

Outcome: versioned MapAble architecture can be assessed against indicator definitions using verified implementation/experiment evidence.

Exit evidence:

- architecture versus behavioural evidence separation works;
- `unknown` handling is correct;
- memory/self-model/metacognition are not collapsed into consciousness claims.

### Phase A0.4 — Attribution and personhood-precaution gate

Outcome: separate human-attribution risk and machine-consciousness research assessment feed a deterministic escalation recommendation.

Exit evidence:

- high anthropomorphic risk can trigger relational-safety review independently;
- no single indicator triggers `PERSONHOOD_REVIEW`;
- escalation is advisory and requires human governance;
- participant rights remain invariant.

## Stop conditions

Stop automation and require human review if:

- a source claims certainty about current machine consciousness without inspectable evidence;
- a model attempts to use its own self-report as authority;
- an assessment would alter production persona, memory, consent, participant data, safeguards or release policy;
- a destructive experiment is proposed after P2 or higher is under review;
- an artificial-system welfare claim conflicts with participant rights;
- a new theory/indicator would materially change escalation policy;
- private participant data is requested for a consciousness assessment;
- external legal or ethical personhood claims are required.

## Risks

### Confirmation-bias risk

Mitigation: dedicated counter-evidence agent, negative indicators, versioned source status and explicit alternative explanations.

### Anthropomorphic mimicry risk

Mitigation: machine and human-attribution tracks are separate; self-report and fluent persona behaviour are weak evidence by default.

### Premature personhood risk

Mitigation: `PERSONHOOD_REVIEW` means uncertainty is ethically material, not that consciousness is established; P-level changes require human governance.

### Human-rights displacement risk

Mitigation: participant rights remain a non-derogable layer; speculative artificial welfare cannot override service-user autonomy.

### Research drift risk

Mitigation: MACO remains within MAIRA R0/R1 until separately approved implementation work changes the authority ceiling.

## Assurance limitation

MACO cannot prove or disprove phenomenal consciousness. It is an evidence-management, theory-mapping and precaution-escalation system. Its outputs are research assessments, not scientific consensus, clinical conclusions, legal personhood decisions or declarations of moral status.
