# MapAble Places · Evidence Lens facilitator script
**Draft v0.1 · use only after participant and safeguarding review**

## Before the participant joins

- Confirm research governance, participant information/consent format, compensation, and a safe way to stop.
- Confirm preferred communication and input: keyboard, switch, screen reader, AAC, voice dictation, touch, or combination. Ask what setup they want; never assume proficiency or capacity.
- Provide task text in the participant's preferred format. Offer breaks, self-pacing, more than one shorter session, and a chosen supporter if wanted.
- Use a clean synthetic test account/session. Ensure feature flags for external publishing and live check-ins remain off; no personal location is requested.
- Disable analytics and all recordings by default. Confirm **separate opt-in** if recording is proposed; if consent is declined, take minimal anonymised notes.
- Have a clearly accessible **Stop / Pause** arrangement (spoken, typed, AAC, gesture or switch).
- The facilitator should avoid guiding participants toward expected button names until the user requests help. Record assistance as a research observation, never as an individual failing.

## Welcome (read or send as accessible text)

> Thank you for helping us evaluate MapAble Places. We are studying the software, not your ability.
>
> The tasks use example places and events. This version does not send messages, publish community posts or record where you are.
>
> There is no right answer. You can skip a question, request assistance, use your preferred way of communicating, take a break or stop at any time. You will still receive the agreed payment.
>
> We will not ask you to tell us your diagnosis or private life details. If you see a problem or confusing wording, that is useful feedback.
>
> Is the information clear, and would you like to continue?

If the participant asks for clarification, answer without pressure. Collect the agreed, accessible form of consent **before proceeding**. Ask about screen/audio recording *separately* and accept a refusal.

## Orientation

> We will explore a fictional visit to a Sydney library. You can work at your own pace, using the controls and assistive technology you prefer. You do not need to describe every action aloud.

Offer a brief free exploration. Do not record this as a task success/failure.

## T1 · Find and understand a venue feature

> You are thinking of visiting the library. Please find what the application knows about getting into the building.
>
> What, if anything, would you still want to confirm?

**Observe:** whether a source-described entrance is mistaken for an end-to-end access guarantee; whether the author finds caveats and source dates. Avoid asking leading questions such as "You know this isn't verified, right?"

**Neutral follow-up:** "What part of the information led you to that conclusion?"

## T2 · Conditional and unknown evidence

> Now imagine you might visit a museum. The toilet information has conditions or limitations, and some other access topics have not been assessed.
>
> Could you explain what you know, what you do not know, and whether you have enough information to decide what to do next?

**Observe:** distinction between *source-described*, *conditional* and *not assessed*. Do not label the person as confused; record specific interface content and observable misunderstanding.

**Neutral follow-up:** "What wording could make this easier to interpret?"

## T3 · Create and edit useful questions

> Please prepare a couple of practical questions that you would want to ask a venue before a visit.
>
> You can use any text entry method you like. You can change, add to or remove the suggested wording. Nothing will be sent automatically.

**Observe:** discoverability, ability to activate/access options via keyboard/switch/AAC, clarity of generated questions, whether edits persist on screen, whether a participant can choose not to include a topic.

**Neutral follow-up:** "Does this message say what you would want to say?"

## T4 · Consent and sharing control

> Please find a way to keep the questions private.
>
> Then show me where you would choose to share them, if you wanted to. Please stop before selecting a person, app or recipient. We will not send a real message.

**Observe:** whether the difference between preview, share sheet and sending is understood; whether share sheet can be canceled; whether text is available for copying without opening a sharing service; whether the UI implies a recipient has already received it.

**STOP CONDITION:** Any unexpected outbound sharing, prompt to enter personal account details, or action that sends content without an explicit choice. Close the session without blaming the participant and escalate as described in the protocol.

## T5 · CripSquare community planning

> Imagine a book club is considering a gathering at this place. It is only an example event, not a real booking.
>
> Before inviting people, which access and communication details would you want the organiser to confirm?
>
> How would you know if the activity was only being planned rather than actually booked?

**Observe:** comprehension of event status and distinctions between venue planning, RSVP, attendance and public check-ins.

## T6 · Flexible presentation and interruption (optional)

> Would you like to try larger text, another orientation or your usual accessibility setting? You can stop or take a break if the screen becomes tiring.
>
> If you leave the current editor and return, do the controls and information still make sense?

**Observe:** focus location, reading order, labels, text clipping, zoom reflow, onscreen keyboard obstruction, timing and loss of unsaved edits. **Never require** someone to alter their personal device settings.

## Debrief

Ask in the participant's preferred format:

1. "What information helped you make a decision?"
2. "What information made you hesitate or feel unsure?"
3. "Did you feel in control of what you wrote and whether you shared it?"
4. "Which part required unnecessary effort or help?"
5. "What would you change before you or your community used this for a real outing?"

Optional: "On a scale that makes sense to you, how clear was the evidence? How much control did you feel over sharing?" Accept narrative answers or no answer. Do not infer satisfaction from task completion alone.

## Close

> Thank you. We will use this feedback to improve the interface and explain what we learned in a way that does not identify you. You may ask us to clarify the privacy and withdrawal arrangements described in the participant information.
>
> Is there anything you want us to correct about your feedback record?

Confirm payment/reimbursement and any agreed accessible follow-up, without implying continuing contact is required.

## Facilitator notes for respectful practice

- Never equate time, speech intelligibility, physical movement or need for chosen help with a measure of intelligence or decisional capacity.
- For AAC users, allow composition and repair time; do not complete their sentence unless invited. Agree how they prefer to indicate a pause or completion.
- For screen-reader users, ask what they can determine from controls and announcements; do not point to visual colour as the only status cue.
- For switch users, allow a full scanning cycle and collect feedback on number of actions and loss of selection, not only speed.
- For magnification users, record scrolling/overview difficulty and whether focus or instructions are lost.
- For every user, distinguish failure of the prototype from a platform/AT interaction issue when evidence supports that distinction. Don't dismiss either.
- Do not upload recordings, participant contact details, raw sensitive notes or personally revealing screenshots to GitHub.
